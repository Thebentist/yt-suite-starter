## His model

The opening must give **topic clarity + on-target curiosity** (2byPP L7). Curiosity comes from **contrast**, the gap between what the viewer believes and what you show (xnOe L7). Confusion ("comprehension loss") makes people leave, and trust has to be earned in seconds. On YouTube the thumbnail stops the eye, the title says what the video is and why it matters, and the intro confirms and locks the viewer in. He says the spoken hook matters *more* on short-form, where viewers arrive cold (jZCu L127-131).

## Rules

### 1. Write the packaging before the script
**How:** Write the idea as one line (the viewer's pain or desire), lock the title, rough out a thumbnail concept, and note what the viewer will expect after clicking. His order is packaging → outline → intro → body → outro (7I50 L13).
**Evidence:** 7I50 L25-37: the title is "a must", while the thumbnail can stay "loose" [opinion]. Iqy L203-207: his ideas team briefs the title and thumbnail before production [practice].
**Long-form:** This is his YouTube advice, so it transfers whole.
**Test:** The script opens with the title, the thumbnail concept and a one-line "viewer expects:" statement.

### 2. Confirm the click in the first lines, then beat the expectation
**How:** Repeat the title's key words in lines 1-3 (he does it nearly word for word, in about 4 s). Then add one thing bigger than the promise. His core principle is expectations vs reality ("EVR"). MrBeast confirms he's "inside the pyramids", then exceeds it with "unprecedented access".
**Evidence:** jZCu L113-117 [anecdote]; 7I50 L7-19 [opinion]; pNIY L215 gives the short-form "lock-in zone" the same job [opinion].
**Long-form:** Native. In a reaction video, name the clip or trend from the title in sentence 1.
**Test:** The title's main noun is spoken within about 5 s, and some line in the first 30 s delivers more than the title promised.

### 3. One subject, one question
**How:** Sentence 1 must read only one way, for someone with zero background. Then name the one question every viewer should now have ("how is that possible?"). If you can't name it, rewrite. His "four S's" are subject, stakes, speed and super clear.
**Evidence:** onQo L5-35: in "three ways I grew", grew how? [opinion, from reviewing clients]; pNIY L13-29 [opinion].
**Long-form:** Applies to the intro and the title. The title and thumbnail must resolve to the same subject.
**Test:** Three readers each write the question the first two sentences raise, and all three questions match.

### 4. No delay: topic in sentence one, cut the wind-up
**How:** If the topic first appears in line 3, delete lines 1-2 ("speed to value"). Vague teases ("craziest thing I've ever seen") mostly fail. After the edit, cut every sentence that isn't needed.
**Evidence:** 2byPP L13-27 [opinion]; a7Vj L117-119: "avoid throat-clearing openers" showed up in 3 of 4 channels [measured, about 17 winning videos]; 96HQ L103-105 [practice].
**Long-form:** Transfers. The YouTube clock is longer, with first value due within 1-2 min (LmXp), but he still counts the first 30-60 s as packaging (jZCu L7).
**Test:** Sentence 1 contains the subject noun. Delete sentence 1: if nothing is lost, it was throat-clearing.

### 5. Build the hook on contrast: lean, stun, snap back
**How:** His formula (LmXp) has three steps:
1. **Context lean**: the topic plus a reason to lean in (a pain, a benefit, common ground, or a fact that blows the viewer's mind).
2. **Scroll-stop interjection**: a "but…" line.
3. **Contrarian snapback**: an on-topic reversal.

The YouTube intro version runs context → common belief → contrarian take → proof + plan (7I50 L57-81). Contrast can be stated (A and B both said) or implied (only B, since A is known) (2byPP L103-109). Clarity goes in sentence 1 and contrast in the next 1-2.
**Evidence:** LmXp, his Vegas Sphere video (8M): the biggest screen ever built, "but get this", it's the least impressive part, the audio is [anecdote]. 96HQ L123 calls contrast "the root of all great hooking" [opinion].
**Long-form:** Transfers directly, since it's his YouTube intro template. Shape for Ben: "most people think X causes this; it's mostly Y."
**Test:** From the first 30 s you can write A (the viewer's belief) and B (Ben's reveal) in one line each, and B isn't A restated.

### 6. Say the six words, and make them specific
**How:** A hook needs a **subject**, an **action**, an **objective/end state** and a **contrast** (base state vs new state). Optionally add **proof** ("again") and **time** ("in 90 days"), which can move to line 2. Use exact numbers (22 days beats a few weeks), and bigger evidence beats smaller.
**Evidence:** S9Fl L7-35, L75 [opinion, 4 worked examples]; pNIY L101 [opinion]; a7Vj L107, L115: "specificity beats abstraction" held in all four channels [measured, small n].
**Long-form:** Transfers to the title and the first spoken line.
**Test:** Highlight the six words in the first two sentences: at least 4 present and at least one concrete number.

### 7. Point it at the viewer's pain or desire
**How:** Default to "you/your". Frame a need-to-have pain, not a nice-to-have ("if you have acne, try these" beats "three skincare trends"). The dream outcome often hooks harder than the problem. "I" works only if the character is relatable and had no special conditions (a $1,200 sleep machine kills it).
**Evidence:** 2byPP L71-85 [opinion]. 8Vol L9-31 lists five templates (about me / if I / to you / can you / he-she just did) with lead counts per video [self-reported by the creators].
**Long-form:** Transfers to titles, where Iqy calls it the "desire loop". The relatability part flips for Ben: he's the expert, not the peer, so the relatable character should be the patient or the viewer.
**Test:** The first three sentences say "you" more than "I", and the pain or desired outcome is named in plain words.

### 8. Pick the angle with the biggest contrast (six hook formats)
**How:** Draft the hook in several of his formats:
- **fortune teller**: the future is changing
- **experimenter**: a demo
- **teacher**: a lesson
- **magician**: a visual or verbal stun that stacks on the others
- **investigator**: a secret or finding
- **contrarian**: the conventional wisdom is wrong

Any topic fits all six (xnOe L81-89). Pick by contrast and by the visual you actually have.
**Evidence:** xnOe L9-89 [opinion; says he "studied 100 viral hooks" but shows no method].
**Long-form:** Use it to choose the title angle and the intro. Ben's natural fits are investigator, contrarian, fortune teller (new tech) and teacher. Experimenter needs demos, and Ben is talking head only.
**Test:** You drafted at least 3 versions, and the one you chose has the widest A-B gap.

### 9. Earn trust in the lock-in zone
**How:** The 2-4 sentences after the hook are a trust trial. His levers are proof (the "trust anchor": a study, a case, a credential), relatability, production quality and looks. Proof matters most for education. Close the intro with proof plus a plan.
**Evidence:** pNIY L207-239, his "one contrarian idea" [opinion]; 96HQ L107-119, the viewer's "BS detector" [opinion]; 7I50 L79.
**Long-form:** Transfers. This is where Ben's orthodontist ethos goes (pathos open, then ethos). The looks lever is irrelevant.
**Test:** Within about 45 s there is one concrete, checkable proof item and a stated plan.

### 10. Make what's said, shown and written mean the same thing
**How:** The spoken, visual and text hooks must each be guessable from the others. His misalignment example is dental: say brushing prevents cavities, show someone eating candy, put "gum health secrets" on screen (onQo L49). Viewers look, listen, then look back to check (xnOe L101-105). After the hook, match a visual to every sentence ("visual matching").
**Evidence:** onQo L57-71: Zuru (aligned, 2M) vs shipping containers (misaligned) [anecdote]; xnOe L123-157: 15M vs under 100K [anecdote]; 96HQ L9-17 [opinion].
**Long-form:** The thumbnail text, the title, the first line and the first visual all name one subject. Every intro sentence gets a `[VISUAL:]` note, or a deliberate none.
**Test:** Describe each channel in three words for the first 10 s. All three name the same subject.

### 11. Keep the opening simple and staccato
**How:** Short sentences up front, varied later. Use 5th-6th grade words, active voice and no wordplay. Compress (8 words beats 28) without rushing the delivery. Two or three short sentences are fine.
**Evidence:** pNIY L23-29; 2byPP L37-45 (his $30M rewrite); LmXp "staccato"; onQo L155-157 [all opinion].
**Long-form:** Transfers to the intro. The body can open up.
**Test:** The first 4 sentences average 12 words or fewer, and no term goes unexplained.

### 12. Wrap the unknown in the known
**How:** Use everyday metaphors and "cult hopping" (a celebrity, brand or movie as the comparison). Follow the "hawkeye" order, wide context first and then tactics, and find common ground early.
**Evidence:** 96HQ L19-71 (Steve Jobs on keyboards, Ali Abdaal) [opinion]; LmXp, a Taylor Swift example [opinion].
**Long-form:** Transfers fully. It's the most useful tool for an orthodontic explainer.
**Test:** Every technical term gets a comparison within two sentences, and each section opens with why it matters.

### 13. Front-load value, and make the first point new
**How:** Deliver the first hit of value in or right after the hook. A generic first point sends viewers to the timestamps. His ordering trick: second-best point first, best second, the rest descending, so value seems to climb.
**Evidence:** LmXp: the YouTube value timer is 1-2 min; 96HQ L95-101: Hormozi's first tip comes about 12 s in [anecdote]; 7I50 L87-109 [opinion].
**Long-form:** Native.
**Test:** The first new fact lands within 90 s, and point 2 is stronger than point 1.

### 14. Rehook between points; close on a high
**How:** Build each point as context → application/example → framing (why it matters). End each point with a line that makes the next one necessary ("rehooking"). The outro recaps the pain that was solved. Tie any CTA to a pain point ("native embeds").
**Evidence:** 7I50 L111-157 [opinion].
**Long-form:** Native.
**Test:** Every transition opens a loop, and the last 30 s names the pain that was solved.

### 15. Thumbnails: stun the eye, then validate the title
**How:** The viewer's flow is thumbnail (color, face, brightness) → title ("value hunting") → thumbnail again ("visual validation") → click.
- Use 3 elements max, from: color contrast, an emotional face, a graphic, big text or numbers, red circles or arrows, an aesthetic image, a collage.
- The face's emotion is the emotion the viewer will feel.
- Thumbnail text adds to the title and never repeats it ("basically cheating", not "script writing masterclass").
- The graphic shows the end state, the process, a before/after, or the pain.
- Invert the niche's color meta, check it in dark mode, and keep elements big and clear of the bottom-right timestamp.
- Make about 7 and A/B test 3. Baiting the click is fine if the video delivers.

**Evidence:** Iqy L7-9: the same video got 1,700 views, then 1.2M with a new thumbnail [measured, but it also moved to a new channel]; Iqy L13-207 and jZCu L33-75 [opinion]; jZCu L47: "more than 80%" of viewers use dark mode [asserted, no source].
**Long-form:** Native. The thumbnail isn't in the script, but the script's first line must match it.
**Test:** At most 3 elements, no words shared between thumbnail text and title, and the face emotion is named in the brief.

### 16. Titles: short, one loop, high trust
**How:** Short titles get absorbed at a glance. Educational titles open a desire loop (a pain blocking a desire); entertainment titles open an interest loop. Viewers give each package a trust score out of 100, and clarity and design quality raise it.
**Evidence:** jZCu L63-65, L83-105; Iqy L75-87 [opinion].
**Test:** Read in about 1 s, the title names the subject plus a pain or desire.

### 17. Mine your own winners and remix; don't clone
**How:**
- Sort your own videos by outlier score and find the break point, since a few outliers drive the growth.
- Extract the formats and "power phrases" and remix the wording ("scientifically impossible to skip" was in 2 of his top 4).
- Then study small and mid-size creators with the same format, and drop paid-boosted outliers.
- Cross-niche copying fails, and pooling many competitors blurs to the average.
- Kill ideas with no key visual or no new points.

**Evidence:** pNIY L151-179 [measured, small n]; a7Vj L11-13, L49, L83-87, L193-195 [measured]; onQo L117-141; xnOe L113, L157; 7I50 L39-53.
**Test:** Each title or hook draft names the back-catalog pattern it remixes, and the outline has at least 3 points his audience likely hasn't heard.

### Reactions specifically
In one creator's catalog, videos that opened on the reference clip instead of his own framing underperformed by about 60% (a7Vj L109) [measured, tiny n]. But Ava opened on an already viral clip and got 2.3M (S9Fl L65-67). **Rule:** open with Ben's one-line frame (subject + contrast), unless the clip itself states a single subject within 5 s.

## Contradictions and changes over time
1. **Visuals vs words.** Early on, visual hooks are "100 times more powerful" than spoken ones (LmXp) and "it's not what you say, it's the visual" (xnOe L107). In the middle, visuals only need to clear a "minimum viable visual" bar (onQo L89). Late, the text hook is "most important… by far", visuals come last, and talkers win on text plus spoken hooks (pNIY L53, L181-183). For a talking head the late view is the useful one. In long-form, the visual is mostly the thumbnail.
2. **How many hook components.** Three (onQo, jZCu), four with an audio hook (xnOe L93), or three plus a caption hook (pNIY L39).
3. **Questions.** A question hook is a "cheat code" (onQo L35), and "can you" is one of his templates (8Vol L83). His later data says confident statements beat hedged questions (a7Vj L119). Default to a statement.
4. **"I" vs "you".** 2byPP L71-79 says to swap "I" for "you", but 8Vol's "about me"/"if I" templates and S9Fl's model hook ("If I had to grow…") use "I". His reconciliation is a relatable character with no special conditions.
5. **Filler openers.** He opened about 30 videos with "check this out" as a magician stun (xnOe L51) and later called it a test (S9Fl L47). His own delay and throat-clearing rules condemn it.
6. **Best point placement.** Second-best first (7I50) vs "don't hold the good stuff back" (96HQ L97, LmXp).
7. **Thumbnail timing.** In 7I50 he disagrees with "most YouTube experts" and says the thumbnail can stay loose. Iqy L203-205 and onQo L151 want the concept early. Resolution: concept early, final later.
8. **Copying.** "The pros… recycle" (onQo L143) and copy work (S9Fl L125) later become "copy pasting… corny and lame" (pNIY L179), and cross-niche clones fail (a7Vj L11-13). He moved from cloning templates to remixing within his niche and his own data.
9. **Length.** He wants compression (pNIY, 8 vs 28 words; LmXp) but also 2-3 sentences that say it twice (onQo L155; 2byPP L113; xnOe L117 says 2-4 lines).
10. **Faces.** An unknown face can hurt a thumbnail (Iqy L57-61), but a recognizable one wins in a grid where every thumbnail looks the same (jZCu L37). Ben's face is recognizable.
11. **Perception order.** "Visuals before text, text before speech" (jZCu L155-157), "eyes hunt the text first" (pNIY L55), and visual → audio → visual (xnOe L103). None is cited, so treat them as heuristics.

## What doesn't transfer to 10-14 min YouTube
- Thumb-scroll mechanics: the freeze point, visual pacifiers, the magician's "check this out", and "ratchet up the color and motion".
- Short-form text rules: text on screen for about 3 s, two lines max, no punctuation. The spirit carries over to thumbnail text.
- The caption hook, the "get hotter" lever, and the lead-generation framing of desire hooks.
- Sandcastles-specific steps. Use YouTube Studio outliers and Ben's own catalog instead.
