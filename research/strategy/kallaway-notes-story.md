# Kallaway on storytelling and scripting: rules for a TheBentist explainer

Study notes, 2026-09-28. Sources: the 12 videos in `group-story.txt` plus `7I50PECz7SU`, all read in full (`voice/kallaway/transcripts/`; t5Z and 7I50 also in `reference/transcripts/`), about 60,000 words. IDs are shortened; full IDs in the last section. Labels: **measured** = counted by me from his word-timestamped transcripts; **self-reported** = his claim about one of his videos (n=1); **opinion** = asserted without data, which is nearly all of it. Inferred order (from the follower and community counts he states): t5Z and 7I50 earliest, W-- latest.

## What his own videos do (measured, 13 videos, 11 to 33 min)

- Credential line at 0:16 to 0:48 in 13 of 13; the plan ("in this video I'm going to") by 0:40 in 11 of 13.
- Spoken section turns ("All right...") every 95 to 170 s (median gap) in 10 of 13.
- First mid-video self-promotion at 32 to 67% of runtime in 10 videos (median 50%); none in t5Z and jtmst; a product demo from 17% in _Z11. A closing pitch in the last 11% of all 13.
- Explicit list recap ("as a recap", "as a summary") in 7 of 13, always at 88 to 96%.
- "but" 3.8 to 9.5 per 1,000 words (mean 6.1); "therefore" nearly absent outside quoted clips; "and then" 6 to 18 times per video, inside explanations.
- Sentence length (whisper punctuation): mean 13 to 17 words, SD 8 to 11; 12 to 25% of sentences have 6 words or fewer, 9 to 24% have 25 or more. Jagged, not staccato.

## A. Structure

**1. Don't script until the outline says something new.**
How: packaging (idea, title, loose thumbnail), then outline, intro, body, outro. *Law of interesting*: shock is the distance between what the viewer knows and what you say; give each fact a *shock score* (of 100 people, how many haven't heard it) (_Z11). If the outline is common knowledge, research more or drop it (7I50).
Evidence: "if I don't have unique points in the outline I'm not moving forward" (7I50, opinion); one max-shock fact gave "40 million views in five days" (_Z11, self-reported).
Long-form: transfers; 12 minutes needs 8 to 10 reveal-grade facts, not a short's 3 or 4 (SDHKQ).
Test: shock-score every point; if under half would surprise a parent, stop.

**2. Open with confirm, common belief, contrarian take, then proof and plan; cake before icing.**
How: the first line bluntly confirms the title and ideally beats it (*click confirmation*); state the common belief; contradict it; give proof and an ordered plan (7I50). Order what/why, then who/how, then when/where (*jumbling the W's*, wgX).
Evidence: "You lead with the cake." (wgX, opinion); measured: his own credential lands by 0:48 in 13 of 13.
Long-form: transfers whole (bible section 18). In a reaction, the common belief is the clip's claim.
Test: the first sentence alone names the topic; a but/actually turn before 0:30; no date or place opens the video.

**3. Start hot, hit the first conflict by minute two, then pulse.**
How: the *modern story arc*: open at about 70/100, spike to 90 with the first conflict in minute one or two, release to 30, rebuild to 75, repeat every 2 to 5 minutes; the biggest peak may come first or last (wgX).
Evidence: "It starts at about 70 out of 100." (wgX, opinion; his drop-off figures are unsourced).
Long-form: his one explicitly long-form shape; the troughs are where Ben explains.
Test: score each beat 0 to 100: 70 at the open, a peak above 85 before 2:00, no 5 minutes without a peak.

**4. Write the reveals first, back-fill context, order loops by dependency.**
How: *story loops*: context clear enough to predict from, then a reveal. A reveal is worse than expected, equal, better, unexpected-and-confusing, or unexpected-and-intriguing; only better and intriguing hold. Pick the reveals, write the minimum context each needs, order so no loop needs a later one's context (SDHKQ).
Evidence: "Each loop is just a context opener and a reveal closer." (SDHKQ, opinion); a short built this way got "a million views on each Instagram and TikTok" (self-reported).
Long-form: "eight to 10" loops for YouTube (SDHKQ), about 60 to 90 s each.
Test: each reveal has a setup line that makes it believable (none = confusing); a reveal the viewer could guess is "equal": rewrite or cut.

**5. Run every point as a value loop in a fixed pattern.**
How: *value loop*: context (what it is, simply), application (how, with examples), framing (why it matters to the whole) (7I50); later: what's going on, why it matters, example, what to do (9K1b6). Long version: *broad to narrow* (context, stakes, strategy, tactics, examples, principles, W--). A fixed pattern gives *story navigation*.
Evidence: "at any point, you should be able to pause the video and a viewer can tell you exactly where you're at" (9K1b6, opinion).
Long-form: matters more than in shorts; Ben's "what to do" is often what to ask your orthodontist.
Test: at any random paragraph, a listener can say which part this is and why it matters.

**6. Make the first body point new; let value climb.**
How: the first point after the intro must beat what they know; he puts the second-best point first and the best second so value seems to rise (7I50). Later videos push value earlier (W--, p4W23V).
Evidence: "put your second best body point in that first slot" (7I50, opinion, never repeated).
Long-form: keep "point one is new"; the ordering trick is optional; a twist stays last.
Test: read point one to someone who knows the topic casually; "I knew that" means swap it.

**7. Rehook at every seam: close one loop and open the next in the same breath.**
How: seams are the danger zones; add a new question, complication or stake: "which would have been great except", "but here's the problem" (KyC8r). *Loop openers* say the last point mattered and the next matters more, through a contrast word (pcnrz). Or re-aggravate the pain (0f6), or solve one problem and create the next (*rebuy*, wgX).
Evidence: "The rehook is the moment where one loop closes and another loop opens at the same time." (KyC8r, opinion). Named in 8 of 13 videos (measured).
Long-form: matters more (timing, rule 16).
Test: read each section's last sentence with the next one's first; no open question means a dropped baton.

## B. Named devices

**8. Run the addiction loop: stakes, big question, head fake, rehook.**
How: *stakes*: a character to root for, something at risk, urgency ("within a week" beats "eventually"); relevant, not life-or-death. *Big question*: specific enough to predict, never "something crazy happened". *Head fake*: breaks the prediction but is obviously true once heard (the magician shows the empty hat first) (KyC8r). Add a villain, which can be a system (wgX).
Evidence: "A great head fake is one that you would never have expected, but as soon as you hear it, you immediately understand why it's true." (KyC8r, opinion).
Long-form: one big question in minute one, smaller ones per loop. Ben's villain is a documented practice, never a person; the character is often the viewer's own mouth.
Test: for each head fake, name the prediction it breaks and the earlier clue that makes it fair.

**9. Use the dopamine ladder as a checklist, not as science to say on camera.**
How: *stimulation* (first 1 to 2 s: colour, motion, contrast), *captivation* (a relevant non-obvious question), *anticipation* (enough facts to guess), *validation* (a non-obvious answer closes the loop), *affection* (likability, mostly from solving their problem), *revelation* (you become a repeat source of value) (jtmst).
Evidence: "The highest level of dopamine is achieved just before the answer comes." (jtmst, opinion; pop neuroscience).
Long-form: levels 1 to 4 per loop, 5 and 6 across videos; solving real problems is Ben's strongest lever.
Test: for the cold open, name the first frame's motion, the question by 0:10 and where it is answered.

**10. Join beats with but or therefore; split big claims with a contrast word.**
How: *the dance*: context, conflict, context, conflict, joined by but or therefore, never "and then" (t5Z, South Park). *Contrast words* (but, actually, instead, turns out, except, yet): lean them one way, then flip (pcnrz).
Evidence: "but is probably the most powerful word you can use in storytelling" (pcnrz, opinion); measured usage above.
Long-form: transfers whole; ben-rules 8 already keeps the explanation exception.
Test: read only the connectives between beats; "and then" fails; "but" at 4 to 10 per 1,000 words is his band.

**11. Vary sentence length on purpose.**
How: *rhythm*: the Gary Provost passage; one sentence per line, look for a jagged right edge (t5Z).
Evidence: "if you look straight down it'll look like a jagged edge" (t5Z, opinion); his own SD of 8 to 11 words (measured).
Long-form: transfers (the bible's blind test found the same). Ignore "staccato" (0f6).
Test: sentence-length SD of 7 words or more.

**12. Sound like one friend letting them in on something.**
How: *tone*: a voice note to one close friend (t5Z). The *gossip whisper* beats the *town crier* ("huge news") (LvuoNl). Add a *fun wrapper* (p4W23V).
Evidence: "You're sharing something with them." (LvuoNl, opinion).
Long-form: transfers; Ben's voice already does it.
Test: no announcer lines ("huge news").

**13. Make an old topic feel new without lying (the illusion of novelty).**
How (LvuoNl): *new reveal* plus *outcome mapping* (a new angle on an old thing, tied to an outcome they want); *contrast framing* against the belief they hold, as a true opposite ("everyone thinks X, but actually Y"); *urgency*, the "turbo button", only when something really changed; *bullseye proof* on the *trust ladder* (someone like the viewer, then a crowd, then a study); *protect the illusion* ("don't show the mascot"). Cousin: *story lens*, the uncommon angle (t5Z).
Evidence: "every space has old stuff and old stuff always has new angles." (LvuoNl, opinion). His root-canal demo uses assumed facts and an invented patient.
Long-form: his most Ben-specific video (he names dentists). Steps 1 and 2 transfer; urgency only with a date; proof sourced, no "patient like you" unless Ben supplies one (ben-rules 2). A doctor never hides that something is old; Kallaway shows the mascot himself as his finale, the honest version.
Test: the premise holds a belief and its opposite; every proof line has a source; no urgency word without a date.

**14. Layer the story locks where they fit.**
How (pcnrz): *term branding* (name the idea); *embedded truths* ("when you", not "if you"); *thought narration* (say the viewer's objection aloud after a big point); *negative frames* (the *negative flip*: "how to X" becomes "the mistake killing X"); loop openers; contrast words.
Evidence: "It's just one word if versus when" (pcnrz, opinion).
Long-form: thought narration and naming transfer; coined terms keep their plain meaning beside them (bible 16). Embedded truths only for framing: a clinician keeps "might" where the evidence says might.
Test: two or three thought-narration lines in 12 minutes; every coined term glossed; no false certainty on a clinical claim.

**15. Cut everything off the through-line, then say the hard part twice.**
How: *story flow*: is each line needed context or a logic step, or a detour? You needn't use every word in the word bank (0f6). *Atomic messaging*: sixth-grade words, active voice, name things (0f6). Explain once for a colleague, again with a metaphor for a child, and show it (wgX, 9K1b6). Leave one retellable line (*atomic shareability*, wgX).
Evidence: "People do not feel good when they feel dumb." (0f6, opinion).
Long-form: the Magic School Bus bar; "shorter is always better" (p4W23V) means density, not runtime.
Test: every line deletable without the next losing meaning is gone; every technical claim gets a simpler restatement or a visual.

## C. Pacing and payoff placement

**16. Turn every 1.5 to 3 minutes; the first turn by minute 2 or 3.**
How: his stated numbers vary (contradiction 2): 60 to 90 s (pcnrz), first rehook at 2 to 3 minutes and 4 or 5 in all (0f6).
Evidence: measured: his own section turns come every 95 to 170 s.
Long-form: 5 to 8 turns in a 12-minute video.
Test: timestamp the turns in the cut; no gap over 3:00.

**17. Land two real "aha" moments early; place the biggest payoff where the arc needs it.**
How: one aha earns a like, two earn a follow (*double aha*, W--); load the big question early (KyC8r).
Evidence: "double aha straight into aligned CTA" (W--, opinion); measured: that video's CTA lands at 9:50, as he claims.
Long-form: two non-obvious clinical facts by about 40% of runtime.
Test: mark both ahas; both before 40%.

## D. Ending and CTA

**18. Write the last line first and end high.**
How: *direction*: write the last line (the *last dab*) and the first, then fill the middle (t5Z). Outro: remind them the pain got solved, the free chocolate with the bill (7I50), or extend the value off-platform (0f6).
Evidence: "I like to call the last line of my script the last dab" (t5Z, opinion); measured: 7 of 13 of his videos end on a list recap, a pitch, then "Peace".
Long-form: the last dab transfers; his list recap does not (house rule: last line on the viewer's life, bible rubric 24).
Test: the last line alone makes sense at a dinner table; no list read-back.

**19. Put any ask where it is the natural solve, never as a break.**
How: *native embeds*: in the outline, find the point where your resource is the answer; frame the pain, offer it, move on (7I50); ideally after the second aha (W--).
Evidence: "you just want to seamlessly add it in showing it natively and then move on" (7I50, opinion); measured placement above.
Long-form: Ben has no lead magnet and subscribe goes after the story; what transfers is at most one pointer to a published Ben video where it is the solve.
Test: delete the ask; if the paragraph reads better, it was a break.

## E. Workflow

**20. Facts, emotion, hook, structure, draft, filter.**
How (_Z11): bullet the high-shock facts; write the target emotion at the top (*emotional transfer* drives shares); pick a hook format and check the four *hook commandments* (visual, spoken and text aligned; speed to value; clarity; curiosity); pick a story structure; layer facts and transitions; reread through the emotion. Final check: interesting, compressed, hook works alone, emotion at the end. AI for pieces, not whole drafts.
Evidence: "I personally don't really like an AI program writing my scripts end to end." (_Z11, opinion).
Long-form: his "breakdown" structure (hook, context shock, how it works, why it matters, outro) fits Ben's explainers.
Test: the draft carries the emotion line, the scored facts and the chosen structure.

## Against the bible (sections 18 and 19)

**Confirmed.** Expectations versus reality is the spine of the corpus under other names (contrast, five reveal outcomes, contrast words). The intro formula recurs (9K1b6, LvuoNl, W--); the dance returns as contrast words; tone as gossip whisper; the visual hook as stimulation and alignment (Cleo Abram's dinosaur bone is his example, p4W23V); story lens as the new reveal.

**Extended.** Rehooking, his most repeated idea, gains mechanics and timing (rules 7, 16). The value loop gains a four-part version, broad to narrow and story navigation. Story loops add reveal-first writing and dependency order; the addiction loop adds stakes and head-fake fairness; the illusion of novelty adds the trust ladder. Working backwards recurs (SDHKQ, 9K1b6), per reveal, not only for the last line.

**Contradicted or weakened.** Second-best-first is a one-video opinion; later videos push value early. The bible read his recap as one breath; true of 7I50, but later videos read the whole list back, so "no recap" is our rule, not his. The last dab appears only in t5Z. 0f6's "staccato" contradicts rhythm; his own speech sides with rhythm.

## Contradictions between videos

1. Classic arc: "broadly, this is the path that most great stories follow" (SDHKQ) vs "that structure is completely wrong" (wgX). Reconcile: arc at macro scale, loops inside.
2. Rehook cadence: 60 to 90 s (pcnrz), 2 to 3 min (0f6, 9K1b6), 2 to 5 min (wgX).
3. Loop count: 8 to 10 (SDHKQ) vs trust after "three to four loops" (wgX) vs 4 or 5 rehooks (0f6).
4. Dopamine fires on reward (jtmst) vs "not the pleasure chemical, it's the prediction chemical" (KyC8r, later). Both pop science.
5. Head fake: a misdirect before the answer (jtmst) vs the surprising answer itself (KyC8r).
6. Vary sentences (t5Z) vs "short, punchy, staccato" (0f6).
7. "Shorter is always better" (p4W23V) vs his own 11 to 33 minute videos.
8. "you don't manufacture a lie" vs an invented patient in the same video (LvuoNl).
9. Value order: ascending (7I50) vs early (W--, p4W23V) vs biggest peak first or last (wgX).
10. Cut every hedge (pcnrz) vs the bible's finding that humans hedge.
