## A. The algorithm (his model)

**A1. Win the first test sample.** The platform fingerprints the video (transcript, audio, caption), builds a "fit score" and shows it to about 200 predicted fans. Then it nukes, retests or amplifies (200, 2,000, 20,000...). Weak samples come from fake followers, wrong followers or a bad video ("bad sampling").
- Evidence: "The algorithm is a math equation. It does not have feelings." (cAEYGdSnt9M) [O; V13 cites comments from Instagram's CEO]
- Long form: the seed-audience idea carries over. A 9.5M base built on shorts and TikTok compilations is his "wrong followers" case: the sample comes back weak even when the video is good. His fix is 10-20 disciplined uploads to retrain the match (V4).
- Test (channel): in the first 48h, compare CTR and average view duration for subscribers against browse/suggested strangers. If subscribers do worse, it's a matching problem, not a craft problem.

**A2. Hold one topic cluster and say its keywords.** Platforms are "interest media". Put the topic words in the script and description, and run every idea through "avatar filtering": will my ideal viewer care? An off-avatar banger confuses the targeting for the next upload.
- Evidence: "It's not really social media anymore. It's more like interest media." (tVZOS5Z019U) [O]. His mixed tech-plus-marketing account got millions of views while the audience stalled (tVZOS5Z019U) [A].
- Long form: carries over fully. In `research/thebentist/own-longform.tsv`, the lows "I genuinely Cannot Explain This.." (3.9K) and "The internet has gone too far.." (6.1K) name no topic. The 2-10M hits all say TikTok/braces/orthodontist.
- Test (idea): the title and first spoken sentence each carry a cluster keyword.

**A3. Treat duration as the gate and shares as the multiplier.** V1 says share rate (shares/views) is all that matters: above 3% a video goes viral, below it rarely passes about 500K (V6). V4 adds average view duration (AVD). V6 ranks shares > comments > saves > likes > views.
- Evidence: "If this share rate is above 3%, your content will go viral" (ECQB_attJUg) [O, a threshold asserted with no data]
- Long form: shares matter less on YouTube long form. Keep shareability as a design target (C5) and retention as the metric. My addition, not his: CTR and satisfaction signals, which he never covers.
- Test (script): name who a viewer would forward it to and the sentence they'd type when sending it.

**A4. Choose the game before the topics.** "Category virality" means broad reach inside one category, paid by brand deals. "On-target virality" means a narrow slice, paid by products. In the wheel-and-ball picture, a bigger ball (more reach) can't roll down a narrow slice to the high-intent edge.
- Evidence: "Specificity drives dollars, broadness drives virality." (94ezZMYPzls) [O]. His IG account reached 95K followers and 40K emails in 5 months, with no video over 1M "by design" [M].
- Long form: TheBentist is paid by ads and brand deals, so the category game applies. Topics can roam inside the category if each one "match[es] the promise I'm stating in my bio" (V13).
- Test (idea): it keeps the channel promise, "an orthodontist on teeth". If it only fits "weird internet", it belongs in the "max" ring (C4).

## B. Viewer psychology

**B1. Go one standard deviation from the base desire.** Every viewer wants money, time, health or access/status (the "four horsemen"). Naming the base desire trips the BS detector, so package a concrete proxy and let the viewer make the leap.
- Evidence: "anytime I talk specifically about making more money, those videos don't perform very well" (g_6TIu7zHDw; repeated in 7pCEsr-0KIw) [A]
- Long form: carries over. Promise one braces trend or one TikTok claim, never "a perfect smile".
- Test (hook): the first line names a specific thing, not the base desire.

**B2. Get the pain accepted in the first sentences.** "Twist the knife" (state the frustration bluntly). "Audience of one" (*you*, not *I*). Draw the "pain point solution gap" between where they are and where they want to be.
- Evidence: "I use the word you more than any other word in my videos." (g_6TIu7zHDw) [O]. His own hook: you ×9, I ×0 [M].
- Long form: for reactions, the "pain" can be worry or curiosity ("is this safe?").
- Test (script): *you* clearly beats *I* in the first 30 seconds.

**B3. Sprint to trust within 10-30 seconds.** His trust ladder, strongest first: proof for someone like the viewer, proof for yourself, a big following or cosign, having been in their shoes, common ground, production look. "Emulation": look like the viewer's end state. V13: lab coat, filmed in the office.
- Evidence: "The bigger the ask, the more trust required." (g_6TIu7zHDw) [O]
- Long form: Ben holds the top rungs. The risk is saying them late.
- Test (script): a clinical proof line (a case, a number, what he sees in the chair) before 0:30.

**B4. Show a plan with a point of difference.** Contrast what they've heard with your take, then announce the structure. Structure makes viewers feel smart, and they credit you for it.
- Evidence: "If they're lost and confused, they churn." (g_6TIu7zHDw) [O]
- Test (script): the intro has a "most think X, actually Y" line plus a count or map of what's coming.

**B5. Maximize comprehension.** The "light bulb effect": fifth-to-sixth-grade words, comparisons to known things, no intro fluff, visuals that show the sentence being said, stories told wide to narrow, and jargon restated in plain words.
- Evidence: "retention charts are essentially just comprehension charts." (YD2vbag4X8Y) [O]. Viewers leave not from boredom but because "they lose trust that the rest of the video is actually going to help them" (7pCEsr-0KIw) [O].
- Long form: carries over fully. It matters most here, since clinical jargon is Ben's native tongue.
- Test (script): read line by line and ask "am I clear where this is headed?" Flag every clinical term that isn't restated plainly. Aim for two light-bulb moments early.

**B6. Keep loops open and vary the rhythm.** "Create a hunt": lead with the most valuable piece, tease more, and keep one long loop open while mini loops close. Re-hook with a "contrarian flip" or a new conflict ("dance with context and conflict"). "Trance rhythm": mix short and long sentences.
- Evidence: "You want that jagged line." (g_6TIu7zHDw) [O]. His re-hook interval is "20 to 25 seconds" (cAEYGdSnt9M) [O, short form]
- Long form: the loop logic carries over. The 20-25 second number doesn't, and he gives no long-form interval, so use section-level loops.
- Test (script): the intro's long loop is paid only in the final act, each section ends on a new question, and sentence length varies down the page.

**B7. Be likable fast.** Raise energy about 50% (he records line by line), simplify, and don't play a character.
- Evidence: first-timers take "three to five minutes" to decide whether they like you (g_6TIu7zHDw) [O]
- Long form: this counts for more than in short form, because browse viewers decide inside the video.

**B8. Show the fear, tease the fix early, then solve.** Find the collective fear, show it happening to someone relatable, hide it inside a curiosity loop, tease the solution, amplify, solve. Long form can build the fear more slowly than short form.
- Evidence: "establish the fear immediately and then compress the time that it takes to demonstrate the value" (IywTdYKncqY) [O]. The Brick video drove "$10,000 in sales" [M].
- Long form: for DIY-braces damage clips, tease that a fix exists before the worst footage, or anxious viewers churn.
- Test (script): the first "here's what fixes it" comes before the fear peak.

**B9. Close the loop, recap and over-deliver.** End with "close the loop", a recap and an "unexpected surprise" (beat the expectation the title set).
- Evidence: 0 to 65K subscribers "in like two months" credited to "12 out of 10 quality" (g_6TIu7zHDw) [M/A]
- Test (script): the last 45 seconds pay the intro's loop and add one thing the title didn't promise.

## C. Ideas: his science-based method

**C1. Treat the idea as the product.** Idea = topic + premise + hook (V10), or seven "idea Legos" (V9): topic, angle, hook structure, story structure, visual format, key visuals, audio.
- Evidence: "Nothing else matters if the idea is weak." (pirnGDCZD3Y) [O]
- Test (idea): the one-line summary alone pulls you in ("magnetism on the one liner", V4).

**C2. Mine outliers, hold the winners, remix the rest.** Outlier score = a video's views / its channel's average. Filter at ≥5x with ≥0.5% engagement (V9). V6 uses 20x (or 5x across several creators) and 10x. Watch 10-30 channels, break each outlier into its Legos, keep the proven ones and swap the rest ("five card poker"). He usually keeps the topic, some visuals and the audio mood, and changes the angle, hook and format. Log topics daily and structural patterns weekly.
- Evidence: 10 data-backed reps get "more winners than 10 reps based on your gut" (pirnGDCZD3Y) [O, stated as fact with no data]
- Long form: mine the same content type (long-form outliers), because hooks, structures and formats don't carry across types (V9). TikToks stay raw footage. The angle and structure come from 10-20 min YouTube outliers.
- Test (idea): each pitch cites one long-form outlier at ≥5x and lists which Legos were held and which were swapped.

**C3. Score the idea on the virality formula.** Unique idea (a common idea first to market, or an uncommon one) + large applicable audience + unique point of view ("story lens") + world-class hook + compelling story + luck. The sweet spots are common + large + unique lens, or uncommon + large + normal lens.
- Evidence: uncommon + unique lens is "too niche and there aren't enough people that would react" (ECQB_attJUg) [O]. His Messi business-lens video hit 17.5M IG views, and he reports 1 in 6 of about 300 videos passing 1M [M].
- Long form: "an orthodontist's lens on a mass topic" is his common + unique lens pattern.
- Test (idea): place it in the 2x2 and reject uncommon + uncommon.

**C4. Commit to entertainment or education, and pick a ring.** "No man's land" (edutainment) loses to the best of both. Entertainment needs shock, curiosity and a common point of interest. Education needs clarity about the pain plus "targeted shock". The rings are core/inner/outer/max, weighted 40/40/20/0 for conversion (V7).
- Evidence: "If you're in the middle, you will lose." (cAEYGdSnt9M) [O]
- Long form: TheBentist runs both games. He allows switching only when people "watch because of their face". The long-form slump suggests that isn't true there yet, so every video commits to one game.
- Test (idea): answer yes or no to "can the viewer use this right now?", then build the whole script for that answer.

**C5. Build in a takeaway people share for credit.** People share to gain social or professional credit ("takeaway problem", V4; "walk-away value", V5 and V7; "peer share filter", V7).
- Evidence: people share "when they feel like they can earn credibility or social status" (ECQB_attJUg) [O]
- Test (script): one sentence a viewer could repeat at dinner.

## D. Mistakes and fixes

**D1. Run the Content Diagnostic top-down (V4).** *Click*: the matching problem, then packaging (YouTube only). *Opt-in*: idea, hook, comprehension, boredom (speed to value and speed of delivery). *Share*: takeaway, uniqueness. Never fix a lower stage while a higher one is broken.
- Evidence: short form wins more easily because it doesn't "have a thumbnail that needs to be clicked on" (YD2vbag4X8Y) [O]
- Long form: all eight blockers apply. At 4K-75K views, check matching and packaging before touching scripts.
- Test (channel): for each recent upload, mark the first stage that fails (CTR, then the 30-second retention drop, then shares and comments).

**D2. Other killers.** Over-stuffing after the hook (fix: "shortest path to interesting"). Broken logic lines. No re-hooks. Weak visuals, stale formats, bad "visual switching" pace. "Shotgun topics". Low volume. "Spread too thin" (fix: "methodical expansion"). Forcing a signature format, or dropping one that works: "If you have a bell cow, run it over and over." (tVZOS5Z019U) [O]. Ignoring data. Buyer-side mistakes (V5): precision, pain point, solution novelty, walk-away value, consistency.
- Long form: the braces-TikTok reaction is TheBentist's proven series (bell cow).

## E. Packaging-to-content alignment

**E1. Title plus thumbnail must show pain + solve + proof.** Put direct triggers in the title and indirect ones in the thumbnail, with the pain preferred in the title. Add proof ("I studied 100 viral hooks"). Borrow familiar names to widen reach and speed comprehension ("the robot version of the UFC", V13; familiarity, V12).
- Evidence: "usually targeting the pain in the title is best because there's less room for interpretation" (YD2vbag4X8Y) [O]
- Test (package): at least two of pain, solve and proof are visible across the title and thumbnail.

**E2. Align the hook: visual, then text, then spoken, then audio.** Eyes decide first, so find the key visual first and write the line to match it ("hook alignment").
- Evidence: "the eyes comprehend before the ears can hear" (u7Sb-GIdBqw) [O]
- Test (edit): the thumbnail's subject is on screen and named aloud within 5 seconds.

**E3. Pay the promise fast.** Viewers judge relevance in 3-5 seconds and value by 10 (V4). No hard CTAs mid-script, because they "nuke your share rate" (V6).
- Test (script): the first partial payoff of the title lands before 0:60, and nothing is sold before it.

## F. Contradictions and how his view changed

1. **Algorithm.** V1: share rate is everything. V4: AVD + share rate. V6: a five-metric ranking. V10/V13: a sample-group fit-score model. He moves from one metric toward matching.
2. **Virality.** V1 teaches a virality formula. V7: "Do not chase virality." V13: both games are valid, with "seven figures profit on both sides".
3. **Entertainment.** V3 and V5 say avoid it. V10 says it can win if it's the channel's only game.
4. **Narrow versus broad.** V7: zero ideas from the "max" ring. V13: all-narrow "makes the algorithm feel lazy and sleepy", so mix broad and narrow.
5. **Controversy.** V6 avoids it and ends on a question instead. V13 calls comment controversy "the absolute best way" to go viral, with a caveat about negativity.
6. **CTAs.** V6: no hard CTAs in the script. V3 and V13 build around the action. His own videos pitch Wavy World mid-video.
7. **Originality.** V1: hunt uncommon ideas "in uncommon places". V9-V11: let outlier data decide, because gut ideas lower the hit rate.
8. **Idea scoring.** V1 says uncommon + unique lens underperforms. V4 scores novelty *and* uniqueness 1-10, which implies maxing both.
9. **Blunt versus indirect.** V3 says both "twist the knife... bluntly" and go one standard deviation away. Reconciled: be blunt about the proximate pain, indirect about the base desire.
10. **Quality.** V3: "12 out of 10". V4: the last 1% isn't needed. V8: the "quality-quantity accordion" (7/10, then volume, then 9/10).
11. **Story.** V1: story "is actually not that important if you've nailed the others". V10: storytelling is "really easy to learn" next to ideas.
12. **Calibration.** Paid ads: V6 says platforms "will nuke" organic reach. V11 calls it "anecdotal". He became more careful over time.
13. **Reading level.** V6 says sixth grade, V12 fifth grade. V13 says substance sits "one click above" the viewer's sophistication (depth, not vocabulary).
