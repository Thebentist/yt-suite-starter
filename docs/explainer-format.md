# The Explainer Lane: "Huge If True" packaging for TheBentist

Modeled on Cleo Abram's *Huge If True* (8.71M subs; one highly produced episode a month; all-time top: "What If You Just Keep Digging?" 13M, "The Shoe That Broke Running" 10M, "What's Hidden Under Antarctica?" 9.4M, "Something's Happening to the Ozone Hole" 7.2M in two weeks). Read from her channel on 2026-09-16. This document is the series bible the scriptwriter and motion-designer follow when a video is tagged `format: explainer`.

## Two lanes, not one
| | Lane A: Explainer (this doc) | Lane B: React / Test franchise |
|---|---|---|
| Cadence | 1-2 a month, 12-18 min | weekly, 10-15 min |
| Question | one big, debated, future-facing question | "which of these is safe / breaks / works?" |
| Energy | wonder, earnest curiosity, Ben's humor as seasoning | Ben's humor as the main dish |
| Visuals | p5brush illustrated shots as the spine, real footage as evidence | clips, typodont tests, verdict stamps |
| Examples | tooth regrowth, do braces change your face, can teeth heal | Which Halloween Candy BREAKS Your Braces, mouthguards ranked, braces colors |

Ideas that are tests, rankings or compilations belong in Lane B. Forcing them into Lane A packaging produces a title that promises wonder and delivers a list; the audience punishes that. Both lanes share the verdict stamps and the channel voice.

## What makes an episode a "Huge If True" episode
Cleo's stated selection criteria: the topic is **salient right now**, people are **arguing about it from multiple sides**, and it is **important to understand for the future**. Her process is visual-first: she finds the one visual that makes the idea click (a chart, a diagram, a demonstration, archival footage) and builds a *visual abstract* before writing a word of script. Our version: the motion-designer gets the visual abstract before the scriptwriter finishes.

## Title patterns (copy the shape, not the words)
1. **Wonder question**: "What If You Just Keep Digging?" -> "What If You Could Regrow a Tooth?"
2. **The X that broke Y / The big lie about X / The truth about X / The real reason X**: "The Shoe That Broke Running", "The Big Lie About Nuclear Waste", "The Truth About Egg Freezing" -> "The Truth About Turkey Teeth", "The Big Lie About Dentists"
3. **Something's happening to X / What's really happening at X / What's hidden under X**: -> "Something's Happening to America's Tap Water", "What's Hidden Under Your Gums"
4. **X is weirder than we thought**: "Dinosaurs Were Weirder Than We Thought" -> "Your Mouth Is Weirder Than You Think"
5. **Personal-stakes question**: "Can My Dog Live 80 Years?" -> "Can Your Teeth Heal Themselves?"
6. **What I wish everyone knew about X**: -> "What I Wish Every Parent Knew About Braces"
7. **I tested X**: "I Tested NASA's New Space Suit" -> "I Tested Dyson's $500 Toothbrush on Braces"
Rules: under 60 characters, no "Orthodontist Reacts", no colon, at most one capitalised emphasis word. The credential goes in the thumbnail and the first line, not the title.

## Thumbnail
Face (real reaction, not a pose) plus 2-4 words that are NOT the title. Cleo's thumbnails pair a wide-eyed face with a single striking object. Ours: Ben's face plus one illustrated object from the visual abstract (the tooth bud, the profile silhouette, the water tap). Channel palette: navy or paper background, one accent colour.

## What her episodes actually do (from the ozone transcript, not from theory)
1. Opens **inside a scene in the present tense**: "I'm scaling a glacier right now... This... is... Antarctica!" Then three or four **news clips as receipts**, no commentary.
2. **Counterfactual stakes with dates**: "if we hadn't done anything... by 2040... by 2065..."
3. **Personal memory**: "I remember hearing all about the ozone hole as a kid. And then one day the news about it just stopped."
4. One plain promise: "So, let me show you how that happened and what's about to happen next." Nothing more.
5. A **journey with friction and small jokes** as the through-line (the plane, the wind, "there goes my home").
6. The history is told through **a person with a question and a dated room**: "a 30-year-old scientist with a question," "April 5th, 1950, a dinner party." She flags simplification: "I'm paraphrasing here a lot."
7. **Sponsor at about one third**, in her voice, tied to the trip.
8. A **recurring line** ("chose a different future") that returns as the ending ("the next big choice").
9. Sentences are short, plain, and start with So / And / But / In fact. Zero aphorisms. Surprise comes from facts at scale.
Ben's version of the journey is the clinic and the phone: the patient who asked, the colleague he called, the paper he had to read twice. See `docs/voice.md`.

## Episode structure (chapters are always questions)
Her chapter lists are literally the questions a curious viewer would ask, in order: "What is the ozone layer? Who owns Antarctica? What caused the hole? How did we fix it? What's the next big choice?" Ours follows the same rule: every chapter title is a question, and each is a Google query someone actually types (this is also how the video gets Key Moments in search).

1. **Cold open (0:00-0:40)**: "This is [object/place]." Show the thing. One bold, defensible claim about it and the stakes. No greeting, no channel intro. The primary keyword is spoken in the first sentence.
2. **The promise (0:40-1:10)**: what we are going to find out, and the one line: "...and if it's true, it changes [who] forever." Ben's version of "I'm Cleo and this is Huge If True" is a single recurring line, not a logo sting.
3. **The question chain (1:10-9:00)**: 4-6 chapters, each a question, each answered with one mechanism and one visual. Zoom levels move from big to small to big: the mouth, the tooth, the cell, back to the person.
4. **The "wait, that's weird" beat (around 40-50%)**: a genuine surprise the research turned up. Her episodes always have one ("Is that a penguin?!", "Wait, that's weird..."). Ours is Ben's clinic story: the thing he has actually seen that the textbook does not mention.
5. **The skeptic beat (60-75%)**: "Here is why the people who think this is hype might be right." State the strongest counter-argument fairly, then resolve what can be resolved and leave open what is open. This is where the ethos lives; skipping it turns the episode into an advert.
6. **The cutting edge (75-90%)**: what is being tried right now, who is trying it, with dates.
7. **The next big choice (90-100%)**: the optimistic close. Not "subscribe": a real decision the viewer, a parent, or the profession faces. End on the choice, then one specific next-video pointer.

Length 11-13 minutes, which is 2,300-3,000 spoken words at Ben's measured pace of ~225 wpm (see `docs/voice.md`). Estimate chapter timestamps at 225 wpm, then retime from the real transcript after recording. **Superseded for scripts he reads (learned from the bad-breath read, 2026-09-28):** 225 wpm is his talking pace. A script plays at about 140 script words per finished minute, so 10-12 minutes is 1,300-1,500 script words. See `.claude/agents/yt-scriptwriter.md` and `videos/bad-breath-for-good/take/read-lessons.md`. Re-hook at every chapter boundary with the next question spoken aloud, the way he would ask it.

## Voice
Earnest curiosity first, jokes second. Ben's humor stays, but as reaction to facts ("that is a horrifying sentence and I say it to patients every week"), not as reaction to clips. First person: "I went and asked", "I have seen this in my chair". Say "we don't know yet" when true.

## Visual abstract (required before scripting)
One paragraph naming: the hero object (drawn once, reused all episode), the 3-5 diagrams each chapter needs, the one demonstration or piece of real footage, and the colour of the episode. The motion-designer builds the hero object as a p5brush scene first; the scriptwriter writes to it. Every chapter gets at least one full-screen p5brush shot (6-12 s) and one canvas UI element (stat, label, question card).

## Search packaging
- Description: first sentence answers the title question in one line (that sentence is what Google quotes). Then the chapter questions with timestamps. 180-250 words.
- Every chapter question is spoken aloud in the video, verbatim.
- Pinned comment: the one caveat a clinician would add.
