How to Get Rid of Bad Breath For Good
Why Your Breath Stinks (And How to Fix It)

FORMAT
Talking head, sit-down. One setup, one sitting. 8–12 minutes. Keep sections under 90 seconds.

COLD OPEN
No intro, no "in today's video." First words:
"You cannot smell your own breath. That's not an opinion, that's how your brain works. So the uncomfortable question is — do you have bad breath right now, and would anyone actually tell you?"
Then: "By the end of this you'll know for sure, and you'll know how to fix it. But first, where it's actually coming from — because it's almost never what people think."

VIDEO STRUCTURE
1 — Why you can't smell it
Your brain filters constant smells. Why cupping your hand into your mouth doesn't work. Then the test that does: lick the back of your wrist, wait ten seconds, smell it. Tell them to do it right now and actually pause.
2 — What it actually is
Bacteria on the back of your tongue producing sulfur compounds. Not your stomach, not bad luck. Tease: "And there's one place most of it comes from that nobody cleans."
3 — Where it comes from
Back of the tongue. Between the teeth. Dry mouth. Gum disease. Tonsil stones — slow down here, set up that you'll show how to check later. Then the smaller slice that isn't your mouth: reflux, sinuses, medications.
4 — Why what you've tried failed
Sugary mints feed the bacteria. Alcohol mouthwash dries you out, which is a root cause. Brushing harder doesn't help because the source isn't your teeth.
Land it: "If you've been fighting this for years, it's not that you weren't trying. You were given the wrong tools."
5 — The fix
Scrape your tongue, don't brush it — demonstrate how far back and how to beat the gag.
Clean between your teeth daily, including the back of the last molar.
Fix the dry mouth — water, nose breathing at night, check medications, xylitol gum.
Drop the sugary mints and alcohol mouthwash.
6 — Tonsil stones
Pays off the setup from section 3. How to check. Why digging at them with a swab makes it worse. Then the water flosser tonsil mode — demonstrate the setting, angle, and pressure.
7 — The routine
Morning: scrape, brush two minutes, don't rinse
Day: water, nose breathing, gum after meals
Night: floss, water floss, scrape, brush
Weekly: check for tonsil stones
Every 6 months: cleaning
8 — When it isn't your mouth
If two weeks changes nothing, it's not hygiene and no product fixes it, including mine. Gum disease, reflux, sinuses, medication — see a dentist or doctor.

SOMETHING NICE PRODUCTS
Explain a choice you made, don't sell. Problem → why existing products didn't solve it → what you did.
Water flosser in section 6, tongue scraper and toothpaste in section 5, alcohol-free mouthwash in section 4.
Disclose early, one line. Say what each product doesn't do. One per section, never a lineup shot. No pricing or promo codes in the body.

CALLOUTS
Bracket callout right after the wrist test. Subscribe callout right after the "wrong tools" line. Film both as standalone takes.

OUTRO
Under 10 seconds, end abruptly.
Option 1: "Scrape, floss, hydrate. Give it a week. See you in the next one."
Option 2: "Send this to someone who needs it. They'll know why. See you in the next one."

NOTES
Never answer "how do I fix it" before the 6-minute mark. Tease forward at the end of every section. The wrist test is the most important 20 seconds — deliver it slowly and actually wait.
Matter-of-fact, never shaming. Say early that this is common and easy to fix.
Say every step clearly and separately for on-screen text. Patient stories are gold, anonymized.
Overshoot everything. We can trim in the edit, we can't add what wasn't filmed.

FILMING
One setup. Tongue scraper, floss, and water flosser on the desk, spread out. Demonstrate the scraping and the tonsil mode.

EDITOR
On-screen counter for the wrist test. Text for every step in section 5. Section 7 routine as a clean screenshot graphic — that's the thumbnail. Cut hard between sections.

SHORTS
The wrist test. Tonsil stones plus the flush demo. "Your mints are making it worse." Each 1–3 minutes.
